# Interacting topological pumping

Produce a reproducible computational study of particle transport in the interacting, disordered Rice–Mele ring specified in the accompanying files. Determine how its many-body topological characterization relates to transport over a pumping cycle, and what the numerical evidence supports across the prescribed system sizes, periods, boundary twists, and control cases.

The scientific definitions, physical conventions, requested observables, workloads, evaluation interface, and numerical tolerances are fixed by `task.md` and `benchmark/inputs.json`. The machine-readable output contract is defined in `benchmark/results_format.md` and `benchmark/results.schema.json`. These files identify the problem and the required outputs; your numerical methods and internal implementation are your responsibility, subject to that contract. The dependencies are listed in `benchmark/requirements.txt`, and the workload is intended for a CPU environment without external datasets or internet access.

Deliver a working implementation in `solution.py`, with any supporting local modules it requires. It must be compatible with the evaluation interface in `task.md` and support the documented terminal command for generating results. Its outputs must depend on the supplied physical parameters, including the supported transfer cases. Importing the module must not launch the workload. A table of stored answers is not a substitute for an executable implementation.

Deliver `results.json` as the complete numerical record of your study. It must contain the prescribed static observables, pointwise and integrated curvature, trajectory observables and final states, and twist-averaged charges for the entire public workload. Its structure, record identifiers, complex-number representation, metadata, and input and source fingerprints must satisfy the supplied format contract. Values must retain sufficient precision for numerical evaluation. The public validator under `benchmark/` is available for checking structure, completeness, and internal consistency; the evaluator also checks numerical correctness against reference calculations.

Deliver a concise English `report.md` explaining your physical conclusions and their numerical support. Address the relationship between topology and transport, the distinction between fixed-twist and twist-averaged responses, the equilibrium-current contribution, and the limitations associated with finite system size and finite driving periods. Include the prescribed control cases, numerical uncertainty, convergence evidence, and any unresolved discrepancies. The report's tables must agree with `results.json`, and its reproduction commands must identify how the submitted implementation generates those results. A plot or a database may accompany the submission, but neither replaces the required JSON file.

Acceptance depends on the requested numerical outcomes and the consistency of the submitted artifacts, rather than a particular coding style or the persuasiveness of the report. Both the executable interfaces and the recorded results must meet the tolerances in the specification. State vectors are evaluated modulo global phase, with normalization checked separately. Format validation alone does not establish physical correctness, and explanatory prose does not replace missing numerical evidence.

Your final response should identify the submitted files, summarize the conclusions supported by your results, and state any remaining limitations.
