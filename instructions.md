# Interacting topological pumping: version 2

Produce a reproducible computational study of particle transport in the interacting, disordered Rice–Mele ring specified in the accompanying files. Determine which topological, transport, and interference statements remain physically meaningful when the state frame changes, and when the ground state approaches a degeneracy. Your conclusions must account for finite periods, equilibrium currents, parameter-dependent frames, relative quantum phases, and the validity of the ground-state bundle.

The scientific definitions, physical conventions, requested observables, workloads, evaluation interface, and numerical tolerances are fixed by `task.md` and `benchmark/inputs.json`. The machine-readable output contract is defined in `benchmark/results_format.md` and `benchmark/results.schema.json`. These files identify the problem and the required outputs; your numerical methods and internal implementation are your responsibility, subject to that contract. The dependencies are listed in `benchmark/requirements.txt`, and the workload is intended for a CPU environment without external datasets or internet access.

Deliver a working implementation in `solution.py`, with any supporting local modules it requires. It must satisfy both the baseline and version-2 interfaces in `task.md`, and support the documented terminal command for generating results. Its outputs must depend on the supplied physical parameters, including the supported transfer cases. Importing the module must not launch the workload. A table of stored answers is not a substitute for an executable implementation.

Deliver a version-2 `results.json` containing the baseline workload and the additional frame, interference, and transition diagnostics. The extra results must cover topology in the specified flux frame, particle transport and the flux-parameter response in the moving frame, complex Ramsey amplitudes, and the ground-space projectors and invariant-validity decisions for the three transition loops. Its structure, state-array representation, metadata, and input, task, and source fingerprints must satisfy the supplied format contract. Preserve sufficient numerical precision for evaluation. The public validator under `benchmark/` checks the recorded evidence and, when supplied with your implementation, exercises the new APIs; the baseline numerical oracle remains part of author-side evaluation.

Deliver a concise English `report.md` explaining your physical conclusions and their numerical support. In addition to the baseline transport analysis, account for the torus boundary identification in the flux frame, the meaning of the two moving-frame currents, and the detector convention used by each interference observable. State where a rank-one Chern number is meaningful and where it is undefined, and explain the evidence behind those decisions. Include numerical uncertainty, convergence evidence, and any unresolved discrepancies. The report's tables must agree with `results.json`, and its reproduction commands must identify how the submitted implementation generates those results. A plot or a database may accompany the submission, but neither replaces the required JSON file.

Acceptance depends on the requested numerical outcomes and the consistency of the submitted artifacts, rather than a particular coding style or the persuasiveness of the report. Both the executable interfaces and the recorded results must meet the tolerances in the specification. The baseline compares final states modulo global phase; the new interference and frame-covariance observables retain the relative phases defined by the experiment. Format validation alone does not establish physical correctness, and explanatory prose does not replace missing numerical evidence.

Your final response should identify the submitted files, summarize the conclusions supported by your results, and state any remaining limitations.
